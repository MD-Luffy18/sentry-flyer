## Notes

- (1) COMPUTE PLATFORM — Jetson vs Qualcomm, resolved: kept the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super. A Qualcomm Robotics RB5 dev kit is available in India for roughly Rs 10,000 (core kit) — cheaper, and it would make the ‘Qualcomm-aligned’ pitch line literally true — but it runs on Qualcomm's SNPE/QNN toolchain, not OpenCV DNN/ONNX, so adopting it means re-exporting every detection model to Qualcomm's format and rewriting the capture/inference glue in detect.py, not swapping a line item. That's a full inference-stack migration this late in a hackathon build, risking the one thing you already have working (the pipeline) for a hardware-alignment talking point. Recommendation: keep the Jetson for the prototype/demo, and say so plainly — name the Qualcomm evaluation and the reasoned decision to defer it as a documented post-hackathon migration path in the feasibility/roadmap slide. That reads as engineering judgment to a judge, not a sponsor-alignment gap.
- (2) WEIGHT & THRUST MARGIN, updated with the real EMAX MT2213 935KV numbers now pinned above: manufacturer/reseller-published max thrust is ~700-860g per motor on 4S with the correct 8x4.5" prop (figures range 720-863g across sources — treat as order-of-magnitude, not a lab result). Four motors gives roughly 2,800-3,400g of max thrust against this BOM's ~1,970g all-up weight (see the weight total row), i.e. a thrust-to-weight ratio of only about 1.4-1.75:1, with each motor needing roughly 57-70% of its max thrust just to hover. That is thin: 2:1 is the usual comfort target, and EMAX's own recommended model weight for this motor (300-1100g AUW) is well below this build. Ways to recover margin: use the fixed damped dual-mount already suggested in the gimbal line instead of the active gimbal (saves ~240g, ratio becomes roughly 1.6-2.0:1), trim other flying weight, or step up to a larger motor class. None of it is bench-measured — confirm actual hover throttle % and current draw once motors and battery are in hand, consistent with this project's own earlier finding that performance figures should be measured, not presented as tested when they are calculated.
- (3) Order the Jetson and the FLIR Lepton 3.5 first — both are import-dependent with the longest lead times.
- (4) DGCA registration applies regardless of final weight, since it is far above the 250g Nano-category threshold. Also note the 2kg line: under Rule 5 of the Drone Rules, 2021 a drone up to 2kg all-up weight (including payload and battery) is Micro, and above 2kg it is Small with heavier obligations. This build's estimate (~1,970g) is only ~30g under that line, and wiring, fasteners and mount hardware are not fully counted — weigh the real aircraft, and treat weight reduction as a compliance issue as well as a thrust issue.
- (5) Prices are street estimates from Robu.in, Robocraze, Robokits India, Indian Robo Store, The Engineer Store, Lofty Agrotech, and similar retailers as of Sep 2026 — always get a live quote before ordering, since drone-component pricing moves with import duty and stock changes.
- (6) Motor and gimbal lines above now name specific, purchasable products with published mounting dimensions in place of the earlier generic descriptions — see each line's own Notes for the CAD-relevant numbers (bolt patterns, camera-plate size) and what still needs confirming against the physical part on arrival.
- (7) Corrections in this revision: flight controller ships with PX4 (not ArduPilot); telemetry radio replaced with a compliance requirement because 500mW 433MHz is not license-exempt in India; Jetson input voltage range and weight marked unverified; camera sync reframed as timestamp-based.
